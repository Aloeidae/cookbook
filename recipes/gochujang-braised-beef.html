Chuck roast gone fork-tender in a gochujang roux, with toasted Sichuan peppercorns split between the pot and the finish. Three hours of the stove doing the work, ten minutes of yours.

Ingredients

  • For the braise
  • Liquids & seasoning

Method

  1. 01

    Toast the Sichuan peppercorns in the dry pot until fragrant, then crush. Reserve half for the end — that's the fresh, buzzy hit.

  2. 02

    Sear the beef hard in the oil, in batches, until deeply browned on at least two sides. Don't rush this; it's most of the flavor. Set the beef aside.

  3. 03

    Drop the heat to medium-low. Ginger, garlic, and scallion whites in the leftover fat, about 2 minutes — fragrant, not browned.

  4. 04

    Stir in the gochujang, flour, and half the crushed peppercorns and cook 1–2 minutes, stirring constantly. You're making a gochujang roux; it should darken a shade and smell toasty.

  5. 05

    Add the stock gradually, stirring the roux smooth before each addition. Both soy sauces and the Shaoxing wine in. Return the beef, cover, and simmer on low about 3 hours, until fork-tender.

  6. 06

    Lift the meat out and reduce the sauce until it coats a spoon. Vinegar in, salt to taste, beef back in. Top with the reserved peppercorns and scallion greens. Rice, potatoes, or bread underneath — anything that soaks.
