Mongolian
Beef

3hincl. marinade
2servings
●●○effort
Jump to recipe ↓

PF Chang's-style Mongolian beef, reverse-engineered by Jason Farmer: an alkaline soak for tenderness, an egg-white velvet for the crust, and a dark sauce that does all the talking. Marinate at least 2 hours — 24 is better.

Ingredients

  • Dark sauce (makes extra)
  • Beef + alkaline marinade
  • Velvet coating
  • Stir-fry

Method

  1. 01

    Dark sauce first: simmer the bouillon, sugar, and water until dissolved. Off heat, stir in both soys, oyster sauce, and rice wine. Cool. Keeps for weeks in the fridge — you'll only use ¼ cup tonight.

  2. 02

    Slice the flank with the grain into 1½" strips, then against the grain, knife angled 30–40°, into ⅛" slices.

  3. 03

    Dissolve the baking soda in the water, add the soy sauce, and submerge the beef for 2–24 hours in the fridge. This is the restaurant-tender texture. Don't shortcut it.

  4. 04

    Pat the beef very dry. Massage in the egg white and white pepper, then the starch, then the oil. Back in the fridge for 30 minutes minimum.

  5. 05

    Heat the frying oil to 350°F. Fry the beef in batches, 60–90 seconds each — edges browned, centers still tender. Drain on a rack.

  6. 06

    Film a wok with 1 tsp oil over medium-high. Garlic in for 10–15 seconds — no color. Add the dark sauce and sugar, boil, and reduce 30 seconds.

  7. 07

    Beef back in, toss 30–60 seconds to glaze. Sesame oil and green onion, toss 15 seconds more, and out. Over rice, immediately.
