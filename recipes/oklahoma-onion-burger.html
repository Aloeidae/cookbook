The Depression-era burger from El Reno, Oklahoma, as made by Kenji López-Alt for NYT Cooking — the one he says he can't improve, only tweak. Half an onion gets smashed into each patty, and the buns steam in the onion vapor. It needs no condiments.

Ingredients

Method

  1. 01

    Divide the beef into 4 even balls and knead each just a little — squeeze until tacky so the patty holds together on the flip. Flatten to about 3" rounds. Season one side with salt and pepper.

  2. 02

    Slice the onions right before cooking, as thin as your knife or mandoline allows. Do not salt them — salt now and they'll purge their liquid before they ever hit the pan.

  3. 03

    Get a dry cast-iron skillet or griddle screaming hot, no oil. Patties in seasoned-side down, then a loose haystack of onions on each — more than looks reasonable. Smash down hard, pressing the edges thin and leaving the centers a touch thicker. Now salt the onions.

  4. 04

    Let the first side sear until the edges are deeply browned, then flip so the onions are underneath, frying in beef fat.

  5. 05

    Cheese on each patty. Set the top buns, untoasted, directly on the cheese, bottom buns perched on top of those, and cover the pan with a clean towel. A couple of minutes — the buns steam soft in onion vapor.

  6. 06

    Slide a spatula under each patty, stack onto the bottom bun, and serve straight away. Pickles if you must; nothing else.
