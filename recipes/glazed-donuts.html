Yeast-raised, fried, and dunked in glaze while warm — the kind that collapses slightly when you bite it. Most of the time is the dough rising; your actual job takes maybe 40 minutes.

Ingredients

  • For the dough
  • For the glaze

Method

  1. 01

    Stir the warm milk, yeast, and sugar in the bowl of a stand mixer. Add the egg, yolk, butter, vanilla, flour, and salt, and knead with the dough hook 6–8 minutes until smooth, soft, and slightly tacky. Cover and let rise until doubled, about 1 hour.

  2. 02

    Roll the dough ½ inch thick on a floured counter. Cut with a 3-inch donut cutter (or two round cutters). Set rings and holes on parchment squares, cover loosely, and proof 30–45 minutes until puffy — a gentle poke should spring back slowly.

  3. 03

    Meanwhile, whisk the glaze ingredients until smooth — it should run off the whisk in a thin ribbon. Heat 2 inches of oil to 350°F in a heavy pot; a thermometer is not optional here.

  4. 04

    Fry 2–3 at a time, lowering each in on its parchment square (fish the paper out with tongs). About 1 minute per side, until deep gold. Drain on a rack, not paper towels — soggy bottoms are a choice.

  5. 05

    While still warm, dip each donut face-down in the glaze, flip onto the rack, and let it drip and set 10 minutes into that crackly shell. Eat one warm as quality control. Mandatory.
